01 — About Me

The Person Behind the Code

I grew up in Queens, New York my whole life — born and raised. I'm the youngest child in my family. My parents moved here from Guyana, South America and built everything from scratch, which honestly shaped how I think about hard work.

Growing up I was mostly into video games and hanging out with friends. In high school I joined the basketball team and started seeing what really mattered — showing up, being there for people, competing. As I got older, family and real friendships became everything to me.

I started college in 2021. CS was booming and my parents pushed me toward it — and honestly it made sense. When AI started evolving so fast it scared me a little, not gonna lie. But I kept going and I'm almost done with my degree at this point.

Outside of school I trade stocks and NQ futures . It started as curiosity and turned into something I take seriously — reading charts, managing risk, staying disciplined. It's taught me a lot about patience and decision making under pressure.

I've been working at Gap Inc. since 2022 where I use Excel regularly to track sales data and inventory. It's not a tech job but it's sharpened my analytical side and taught me how to work in a fast-paced environment. Before that I worked briefly at Express and back in 2018 and 2019 I mentored kids through the NYC DYCD program — leading projects, teaching younger students, building communication skills I still use today.

People who know me would say I'm goofy, caring, and empathetic — just an overall good guy who shows up for others. That's the most important thing to me.

Part 2 — Design

1. Overall style?
Magazine style — bold oversized text, asymmetric layouts, editorial feel.
2. Color scheme?
Teal (#00897B) and orange (#F4511E) — energetic, modern, and distinctive.
3. Fonts?
Playfair Display (bold italic serif headings), DM Sans (clean body), Space Mono (techy labels).
4. Design reflects personality?
Editorial layout reflects ambition; teal/orange palette is bold and energetic; monospace labels give it a CS feel.
5. Homepage layout?
Split screen hero, then scrolling sections with asymmetric magazine-style grids.
6. Project sections visually?
Magazine grid — first card spans wider, rest fill in asymmetrically.
7. Mobile friendly?
Yes — Flexbox and media queries, hamburger menu on mobile, photo panel hidden on small screens.
8. Visual hierarchy?
Large italic display names → section labels → body text. Orange highlights key info.
9. Consistency?
CSS custom properties enforce colors, fonts, and spacing across all sections.
10. Accessibility?
High contrast, 16px+ body text, semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, aria labels.
11. Icons/images?
Font Awesome icons for skills and social links. Personal photo in hero for authenticity.
12. Inspiration?
Editorial magazine layouts and high-contrast portfolio sites with strong typographic identities.

Part 3 — Interactivity

1. Interactive elements?
Sticky nav with smooth scroll, hamburger menu, hover effects on cards, contact form, resume download, copy email button, back to top button, dark/light mode toggle.
2. Contact form?
Name, email, and message fields. JS validates inputs. Submits via Formspree to stevenseed36@gmail.com. Shows success message on send.
3. JavaScript features?
Smooth scroll, hamburger toggle, Intersection Observer fade-ins, form validation, active nav highlighting, copy to clipboard, back to top, dark mode with localStorage.
4. User feedback?
Hover glows on cards, inline form errors, success message on submit, "Copied!" on email copy, active nav highlighting.
5. Interactivity improves UX?
Makes the site feel alive. Animations guide attention. Form feedback builds trust. Copy email and back to top add convenience. Dark mode gives user control.
